Research Experience

Postdoctoral Fellowship, Institute of Ecology (INECOL; 2025–present)

  • Applying machine learning and evolutionary models to investigate ecomorphological occupation patterns in vertebrates. Student mentoring.

Postdoctoral Fellowship, Arizona State University (ASU; 2024–2025)

  • Population genomics of non-human primates: demographic history and autosomal recombination rate estimation in rhesus macaques, and patterns of X-chromosomal versus autosomal diversity in aye-ayes.

Postdoctoral Fellowship, University of North Carolina at Charlotte (UNCC; 2023–2024)

  • Morphological divergence and convergence between domestic dog breeds and phyllostomid bats. Student mentoring. Microscopy and 3D imaging data collection. Training in building a bioreactor for microbiome studies.

Doctoral Researcher (UNAM; 2016–2022)

  • Taxonomic resolution of a cryptic bat species complex and assessment of covariation between morphological and ecological variables using geometric morphometrics, ecological niche modeling, and phylogenies. Elevated four subspecies to species level based on these findings.

Candidature essay: Use of morphological, molecular, and niche characters in evolutionary studies and their integration for phylogenetic inferences and species delimitation in bats.

Thesis: Diversification of Glossophaga soricina in Mesoamerica.

Master Researcher (UNAM; 2013–2015)

  • Evolution of plant defense traits (trichomes and tropane alkaloids) in Datura stramonium across native and introduced ranges. Found that plants that evolved in the absence of specialized herbivores invest up to ten times less in defense traits.

Thesis: Adaptive evolution during the invasion of novel environments: changes in anti-herbivore defenses in Datura stramonium.

Undergrad Researcher (UNAM; 2011–2012)

  • Developed an educational program to teach the model of natural selection in high school, aiming to provide students with the scientific literacy needed to make informed decisions in everyday life. PAPIME intern, project “Learning Evolution”.
